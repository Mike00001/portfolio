Crypto Quantum

The impact of quantum computing on cryptography

Shor's algorithm threatens RSA and ECC. Discovery of post-quantum cryptography and PQC standards.

Today, every time you browse via HTTPS, connect via SSH, or send an email, your security relies on math problems that are unsolvable for classical computers (integer factorization or discrete logarithm). This is the foundation of asymmetric cryptography (RSA, Diffie-Hellman, Elliptic Curves).

Shor's Algorithm: The Destroyer of Asymmetry

In the mid-90s, Peter Shor developed a quantum algorithm that solves these mathematical problems exponentially faster. When a functional quantum computer with enough stable qubits emerges, an attacker equipped with Shor's algorithm will be able to decrypt a 2048-bit RSA message in a few hours, where a classical supercomputer would take billions of years.

The "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later"

Why worry when 'Q-day' quantum computers might only be expected in 5, 10, or 15 years?

Because many intelligence agencies are already practicing "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later". All encrypted HTTPS communications exchanged today between governments or multinational corporations are potentially collected and stored. The day a quantum machine becomes available, all these archives will be retroactively compromised.

PQC (Post-Quantum Cryptography)

The NIST launched a call a few years ago to standardize new asymmetric algorithms resistant to quantum computers. These algorithms are based on different mathematical principles, including:

  • Lattice-based cryptography - e.g., Kyber (for key transmission) and Dilithium (for digital signatures).
  • Multivariate cryptography.
  • Error-correcting codes, etc.

The deployment of the Kyber-768 algorithm has already begun. Google Chrome, Signal, and Cloudflare are currently implementing hybrid modes (combining a classical algorithm like X25519 with Kyber) to protect themselves from the future, without abandoning the current proven security level.
